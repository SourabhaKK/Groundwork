## 1. Business Model Summary

> ### 🟡 MEDIUM
> **4 sources including the Companies House register and 1 named trade-press outlet — downgraded from HIGH by a MINOR conflict over whether the headline FY2026 profit figure is statutory or adjusted.**

Monzo Bank Limited is a UK-registered bank, incorporated 18 February 2015 and currently **Active** on the Companies House register, with its registered office at Broadwalk House, 5 Appold Street, London EC2A 2AG. It files under two SIC codes: **64191 (banks)** and **64999 (financial intermediation services not elsewhere classified)** [1].

The company makes money as a deposit-taking retail and business bank. Its own FY2026 annual report (year ended 31 March 2026) reports **revenue of £1.7bn, up 39%**, with gross profit exceeding £1bn for the first time [2], a figure independently reported by UK Technology News on 19 May 2026 [3]. Customer deposits reached **£25.7bn, up 55%** [2] — deposits are the funding base, so this figure is the closest single proxy for the balance-sheet scale behind the revenue.

Its customers are predominantly UK retail, with a fast-growing business segment. Monzo reports **15.2 million total customers** after adding 3 million in the year, of whom **10.4 million are monthly active** [2][3]. Revenue quality is improving on two measures an underwriter would care about: **1.6 million customers now pay for a subscription plan, up 49%** [3], and the FY2026 revenue split spans multiple streams rather than a single dependency, with borrowing identified as one of them [2].

On the business-banking side, Monzo reports **45% growth in business banking customers in FY2026** [2]. An independent sector analysis put the base at approximately **700,000 business customers as at 2025**, alongside a tiered freemium product (free Lite, £9/month Pro, £25/month Team) and credit products — loans and overdrafts for sole traders and limited companies — launched across 2024–2025 [4].

**⚠️ Conflict detected — MINOR (drives the MEDIUM band).** Monzo's own annual report labels the £172.6m headline profit figure explicitly as **"adjusted profit before tax"** and does not surface a statutory equivalent [2]. UK Technology News reports the identical number as **"profit before tax"**, without the adjustment qualifier [3]. Both sources agree on the core fact — Monzo was profitable in FY2026 and profit grew ~20% — so this is a labelling divergence on a peripheral detail, not a contradiction of substance, and is classified MINOR. It is not, however, immaterial to a credit decision: the distinction between adjusted and statutory profit is precisely where non-recurring costs are excluded, and an underwriter should treat £172.6m as an adjusted figure until the statutory number is confirmed from the filed accounts.

**⚠️ Evidence gaps, stated rather than smoothed over:**

- **The FY2026 figures above are company self-disclosed and are not yet on the public register.** Companies House shows the most recent *filed* accounts as those for the year ended 31 March 2025; accounts for the year ended 31 March 2026 are not due until 31 December 2026 [1]. Every FY2026 number in this section therefore rests on Monzo's own publication, not an independently filed document.
- **No absolute FY2026 business-customer count could be verified.** The 45% growth rate [2] and the ~700,000 base figure [4] come from different sources with different vintages and do not reconcile to a stated FY2026 total.
- **No statutory profit before tax, and no lending-book size, was verifiable** from any retrieved source.

## 2. Competitive Landscape Assessment

> ### 🟢 HIGH
> **3 core claims resting on 2 primary company disclosures and 1 named trade-press outlet. Scope limit stated in the first paragraph below.**

**Scope limit, up front:** this assessment is grounded on the Monzo–Starling comparison, where both companies publish audited primary figures. The wider UK SME-banking share picture rests on a single secondary estimate and is flagged as a gap rather than asserted.

**Competitive intensity in Monzo's core market is high and structurally rising, but Monzo is currently winning the growth contest within it.** The decisive evidence is the divergence between the two most directly comparable UK digital banks in the same reporting period. For the year ended 31 March 2026, Monzo grew revenue **39% to £1.7bn** [1]. Over the identical twelve months, Starling Bank's group revenue **fell to £887.4m from £940.0m** [2]. Monzo is now roughly twice Starling's size by revenue and moving in the opposite direction.

**Why that matters to a lender rather than to a market analyst:** it means Monzo's growth is not simply the whole sector rising together. In a rising sector, a lender can discount a borrower's growth as beta. Here the nearest comparable operator shrank in the same period, which makes Monzo's expansion company-specific — a stronger signal about management execution and product-market fit than the headline growth rate alone conveys.

**The picture inverts on profitability, and this is the material qualification.** Starling reported **profit before tax of £217.1m** for FY2026 [2] against Monzo's **£172.6m adjusted** profit before tax [1] — despite roughly half the revenue. Starling also reports a pre-tax return on capital employed of 34.6% and describes FY2026 as a fifth consecutive year of profitability [2]. The competitive read is therefore not "Monzo is winning" but **"Monzo is buying share, and a smaller competitor converts revenue to profit more efficiently."** For an underwriter, that is the more useful framing: it locates the risk in whether Monzo's growth spend converts to durable margin, rather than in whether it can grow at all.

**Intensity is set to increase on two fronts.** Monzo is actively expanding into Europe, with its CEO describing the intent to bring the product "to Europe in a way that feels truly local from day one" [3] — which puts it into markets with incumbent digital banks rather than the UK's relatively settled challenger set. Domestically, the SME segment is the contested ground, with Monzo, Starling and Revolut pursuing differentiated strategies: Monzo on freemium tiering and tax automation, Starling on a partnership marketplace and banking depth, Revolut on multi-currency and cross-border [4].

**⚠️ Evidence gaps:**

- **No reliable comparative SME market-share figure was obtainable.** A single secondary source estimates roughly 700,000 Monzo versus ~500,000 Starling business customers [4]; Starling's own FY2026 release **does not disclose a business-account total at all** [2], reporting only 6.2 million platform accounts and that 56% of SME customers use it as their primary bank. The comparative SME claim is therefore one unverified secondary estimate against a primary non-disclosure, and should not be relied on.
- **Revolut was not independently assessed.** It appears only in a secondary strategic comparison [4]; no Revolut primary financials were retrieved, so no claim is made about its scale or trajectory.
- **No sector-wide concentration or share data** (e.g. from UK Finance or the CMA) was retrieved, so "intensity is high" is an inference from the three-way comparison above, not a measured market statistic.

## 3. Company Quality Signals

> ### 🟢 HIGH
> **Signal strength: strong. 5 claims across an FCA enforcement notice, the Companies House register, and a review-aggregation source. No conflicts detected.**

**Read this band correctly: it rates the strength of the evidence, not the favourability of the finding.** The most significant signal in this section is negative and is HIGH confidence precisely because it is documented in a regulator's own enforcement notice.

**Regulatory: a material adverse finding, formally resolved.** On 8 July 2025 the FCA fined Monzo Bank Ltd **£21,091,300** for inadequate anti-financial-crime systems and controls between **October 2018 and August 2020** [1]. Two aspects go beyond an ordinary control weakness. First, the FCA found Monzo onboarded customers on "limited, and in some cases, obviously implausible" information — its published example being customers using well-known London landmarks as their home address [1]. Second, and more seriously for a lender assessing governance, Monzo **breached an FCA-imposed restriction**: after being barred in August 2020 from opening accounts for high-risk customers, it opened **more than 34,000 such accounts** between August 2020 and June 2022 [1]. The fine was reduced 30% from £30,130,475 for agreeing to resolve the matter [1].

**The mitigating context is equally documented.** The FCA's own notice records that Monzo "has established and completed a financial crime change programme to remediate and enhance its wider financial crime control framework" [1]. The conduct is also historic — the failings end in June 2022, four years before this report — and the FCA characterised the root cause as controls failing to keep pace with customer and product growth [1], which is a scaling failure rather than an absence of intent to comply. An underwriter should weight this as a resolved governance episode with a documented remediation, not as an open enforcement matter.

**Filing discipline: clean.** Companies House shows the company **Active**, with accounts filed for the year ended 31 March 2025 and the next set not due until 31 December 2026 [2]. No overdue filings and no adverse status markers appear on the register.

**Customer sentiment: strongly positive, but the evidence is second-hand.** A review aggregation last updated June 2026 reports Monzo at **4.6/5 on Trustpilot from 68,000+ reviews** and **4.5/5 on Google Play from 170,000+ reviews** [3]. Recurring praise centres on app design, onboarding speed and the Pots savings feature; recurring complaints centre on **payment problems, account freezing, high overdraft charges, and account-creation issues** [3].

**No conflict is recorded between the regulatory finding and customer sentiment, and this is a deliberate classification rather than an oversight.** Under the Phase 5.4 definitions, a MAJOR conflict requires sources to contradict each other on *the same specific claim*. A regulator's finding about historic AML controls and a review platform's aggregate customer-experience score are claims about different things; they are complementary signals, not opposing ones. Flagging them as a conflict would have forced this section to LOW and thereby *suppressed* a well-evidenced negative finding — the opposite of what the confidence mechanism is for.

**Worth an underwriter's attention:** the "account freezing" complaint theme [3] is plausibly the customer-facing surface of the very same financial-crime controls the FCA required Monzo to tighten [1]. This is a hypothesis suggested by the juxtaposition, not a documented causal link, and is flagged as such.

**⚠️ Evidence gaps:**

- **The review data is second-hand.** Trustpilot and the Google Play listing were not directly accessible during retrieval (both returned HTTP 403), so scores and volumes come from an aggregating commercial review site [3] rather than the platforms themselves. Figures should be treated as approximately, not exactly, current.
- **No business-customer-specific sentiment was isolable.** The cited source explicitly notes the Trustpilot score blends personal and business banking [3].
- **No credit-rating, audit-opinion, or PSC/beneficial-ownership data** was retrieved for this mock.
